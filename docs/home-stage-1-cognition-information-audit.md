# Home Stage 1 — cognition and information audit

**Scope and date:** authenticated `/dashboard`, 2026-09-22. This is a diagnostic product-architecture report, not a proposed layout or priority algorithm. The local demo is fictional and expires after 24 hours; findings about real candidates require validation.

## 1. Executive finding

Home has useful information, but it asks the candidate to reconcile several independently produced claims about what matters. The header chooses one action, “Coming next” provides another question and several links, Action Center groups tasks, and Progress Story offers a separate “What deserves attention” focus. These are not simply repeated cards: their sources, thresholds, scopes, and ordering differ. The working hypothesis—**too many simultaneous claims to priority**—is supported by source and by one observed high-volume demo session. It does not follow that the page should contain less information overall. Some apparent repetition is useful orientation; the defect is chiefly unclear authority and timing. Several attractive Home promises, notably “what changed since last visit” and “the single most important thing,” exceed current data capability.

## 2. Home's core job

**Retain, with one qualification:** Home should restore situational awareness of the *recorded* search and help the candidate choose a defensible next step, while making the limits of that judgment clear. “Confident” must mean confidence in the evidence, not product certainty about an unrecorded personal priority. Home is not the system of record for application edits (Applications and individual opportunity workspaces), interview workflow (Interviews), historical analysis (Analytics), or preference management (Settings). It may point into those surfaces without reproducing their full work.

## 5. Runtime behavior map

**Observed live high-volume path:** header orientation, range selector and “Review what needs you” are above the first fold; the tour is visible; Recent Movement begins at the fold; “Coming next,” Action Center, and Progress require scrolling. The live session's header said “Interviews are shaping this chapter,” while the primary action pointed to overdue work (9 items), and Coming next showed an interview. This is a valid distinction only if the interface clearly separates *search chapter*, *scheduled commitment*, and *task urgency*. The current wording requires the candidate to do that reconciliation. The Action Center initially summarized 10 actions and was collapsed; opening it exposed three groups and individual Complete/Reschedule controls. Progress simultaneously presented a performance interpretation and an overdue-follow-up focus.

**Source-confirmed other states:** `<5` submissions selects foundation copy and, without overdue/interview, Add application; 0 actions shows “You are caught up”; no interview shows an explicit no-interview message; no recent applications shows an empty message; analytics failure leaves Dashboard milestones and retry; dashboard failure replaces the Home body with an ErrorPanel. **Constructed probe limitation:** zeroing only the dashboard endpoint produced “0 tracked” beside stale Analytics fixture milestones. It is not a realistic all-zero user state; it reveals cross-request consistency risk.

**Not reproduced:** actual naturally low-data, quiet/waiting, returning-after-absence, and interview-soon cases. Source and tests support their branches, not their experienced cognitive impact. The existing visual/browser suite covers Home route rendering; [`backend/tests/integration/test_insights.py`](../backend/tests/integration/test_insights.py), [`backend/tests/unit/test_dashboard_timezone.py`](../backend/tests/unit/test_dashboard_timezone.py), and [`backend/tests/unit/test_progress_narrative.py`](../backend/tests/unit/test_progress_narrative.py) cover selected backend facts; they do not establish candidate comprehension.

## 6. Candidate state model

- **New / low-data** (source-confirmed): goal is learning what to record and what the workspace can infer. Highest value: scope, first concrete recording step, candid “not enough history.” Secondary: conversion rates. Dangerous omission: implying a meaningful trend from no sample. Tour is relevant, but should not masquerade as an urgent task. Uncertainty: intent and external applications are unknown.
- **Active applicant** (inferred from product model): goal is identifying candidate-owned commitments across records. Highest value: dated next steps, responsible party, near interviews and state. Secondary: raw count and broad trend. Danger: hiding an actionable commitment behind generic exploration. Analytics is optional unless a pattern affects strategy.
- **Interview-stage** (live with two upcoming): goal is time-sensitive preparation and follow-up with preserved opportunity context. Highest value: next interview, readiness and candidate-owned follow-up. Lower value: generic Add application. Danger: treating every future interview as immediate or letting the “chapter” copy override overdue work. Home should summarize, Interviews should own preparation.
- **Returning** (not reproduced): goal is reorientation, changed commitments, and resuming prior work. Current “Recent Movement” is not since-last-visit; it is only recently updated records. Dangerous omission: a material event that happened before a later trivial edit. Needs a trusted visit boundary or explicit non-visit wording. Tour may or may not be complete; session storage is not durable across all returns.
- **High-volume** (live): goal is triaging without inspecting every record. The Action Center's collapse and three-item group previews bound scanning, but the initial collapse puts actual work below multiple other claims. Danger: total counts and “Overdue” group semantics may imply stronger rank than evidence supports. Analytics may be useful strategically, not necessarily first.
- **Quiet / waiting** (source-confirmed branch, not runtime-reproduced): goal is assurance that no candidate action is due and visibility of what depends on employers. “No immediate attention” can be a healthy state. Dangerous omission: who owns the next move; dangerous commission: manufactured urgency through generic actions or recommendations. Trends are optional context, not a substitute task.

The promising principle is **stable questions, adaptive emphasis**. Candidate state can change emphasis or explanation, but the roles of fact, action, history, and exploration should not change unpredictably. This is directionally supported, not a tested preference.

## 7. Candidate question hierarchy

The supplied order needs one adjustment: consequence and ownership must precede broad progress. A defensible information order—not a screen layout—is: (1) Am I in the right workspace and what is its recorded scope? (2) Is a dated commitment or exception approaching or past? (3) Is the next move mine, an employer's, or unknown? (4) What candidate action is available and what evidence makes it relevant? (5) What material change needs reorientation, *if* that can be known? (6) What is the broad trajectory and evidence quality? (7) What can I choose to explore or edit elsewhere? A genuine no-action state is an answer to steps 2–4, not a gap to fill with a synthetic task. This ordering is a Stage 2 input, not a proposed UI sequence.

## 8. Current information inventory

Each entry states: **question / purpose; source and epistemic type; importance–urgency–frequency; state and hiding cost; appropriate ownership; current issue.** Importance and urgency are conditional, not universal.

1. **Workspace/demo identity:** Which search is this? Orientation and trust; protected session/`WorkspaceFrame`, fact; high importance, low task urgency, every entry; all states, high cost if confused across workspaces; Home/global shell; should disclose temporary-demo boundary, not imply account permanence.
2. **Welcome and journey chapter:** Where am I? `DashboardPage` branch over counts/trend/interviews/actions, inference; medium importance, low urgency, each entry; most states, modest hiding cost; Home contextual orientation; branch precedence may describe interviews while overdue dominates action.
3. **Range selector:** What period do rates mean? Settings + local range, fact/control; high interpretive importance, low urgency, when reading progress; analytics-minded states, low cost if Progress is not salient; Home contextual; mixes range-scoped rates with current whole-workspace action state.
4. **Header primary action:** What should I do now? Client precedence, recommendation/action; potentially high importance and urgency, each visit; all states, high cost if it points away from real commitment; Home; could disagree with other recommendation systems.
5. **Total tracked:** How much is stored? Status count, fact; low-to-medium importance, no urgency, occasional; new/high-volume, low hiding cost; Home orientation or Applications; archived inclusion can be read as current workload.
6. **Active opportunities:** How much is in play? Backend active status count, derived fact; medium importance, low urgency, each reorientation; active/high-volume, moderate hiding cost; Home; count cannot express workload or ownership.
7. **Interviews coming up count:** Are conversations scheduled? Capped backend list count, derived fact; potentially high importance, urgency depends on dates, each visit; interview-stage, high hiding cost near deadline; Home summary, Interviews detail; count is capped at five and may understate more than five.
8. **Needs-attention count:** Is work due? UI Overdue+Today group count, derivation; high conditional importance/urgency, each visit; active, high hiding cost; Home; excludes Upcoming while Action Center badge includes all; Overdue includes low-priority stale records.
9. **Search tour progress/next step:** How can I learn this demo? Browser session-state fact and exploration suggestion; medium importance for new visitor, no inherent urgency, early visits; new/demo, low hiding cost after familiarity; temporary contextual Home help; appears before actual task queue when undismissed.
10. **Recent touched records / timestamps / statuses:** What moved? Server `updated_at` order, fact about edits, not meaningful change; medium relevance, usually low urgency, returning visits; high cost only if misread as a true delta; contextual Home or Applications; “What changed recently” overclaims what the data proves.
11. **Next conversation identity/time:** What commitment is next? First upcoming interview, fact; high conditional importance, urgency rises with proximity, each visit; interview-stage, high hiding cost; Home summary / Interviews work; also appears in count and sometimes Action Center.
12. **No interview state:** Is absence meaningful? Empty schedule fact; low urgency, often useful reassurance; new/quiet, low hiding cost; Home contextual; pipeline link may invite unrelated work.
13. **Keep-search-moving summary:** Is anything due? Overdue/today/none from UI group counts, derived state; high when true, low when none, each visit; active/quiet, medium cost; Home; repeats header and Action Center, can imply all overdue work is the clearest step.
14. **Generic continuation links:** What else can I do? Fixed Add/Pipeline/Patterns routes, optional exploration; low urgency, occasional; all states, low hiding cost; contextual or dedicated routes; adjacency to “What should I do next?” can promote them as recommendations.
15. **Action Center total/collapse:** How much work exists? Server actions count + session preference, derived state/control; high conditional importance, each visit; high-volume, high cost when urgency hidden; Home; 10-item live queue initially summarized but not shown.
16. **Overdue follow-ups:** Did a recorded date pass? Saved `follow_up_date` vs workspace-local today, derived from fact; high urgency if candidate-owned and consequential, daily; active, high hiding cost; Home/action then application detail; date is reliable, importance is not fully known.
17. **Today follow-ups and undated candidate steps:** What is due today? Saved date OR synthetic local-today assignment for candidate responsibility without date, two different derivations; conditional urgency, daily; active, high hiding cost for true dates; Home; undated items should not be treated as factual deadlines.
18. **Interview-soon actions:** Should I prepare now? Scheduled interview ≤24h, threshold inference; high potential urgency, daily; interview-stage, high hiding cost; Home to Interviews/application; threshold differs from Interviews' 4h imminent workflow.
19. **Stale application actions:** Has a stage aged? APPLIED/SCREENING ≥14 days, heuristic inference; medium importance, not necessarily urgent, occasional; active/quiet, moderate hiding cost; Home contextual or filtered Applications; UI groups it as Overdue although it has no deadline.
20. **Action priority/order:** Which item first? Backend HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW then due key, inference; high decision importance, every task review; high-volume, high cost if misleading; Home; priority doesn't know opportunity value, reversibility, external commitments.
21. **Follow-up Complete/Reschedule and pending feedback:** Can I close/shift work? Canonical backend mutation and transient UI state; high when relevant, immediate; active, high hiding cost when task visible; Home allowed, application detail owns full record; eventual index lag may briefly disagree with updated count.
22. **Progress headline/tone:** How is my search moving? Server comparison heuristic, inference; medium strategic importance, low daily urgency, periodic; active/returning, low short-term hiding cost; Home summary / Analytics evidence; can compete with operational priority.
23. **Milestone path and rates:** What has happened? Server-owned historical counts/rates, derived facts with denominator; medium importance, low immediate urgency, periodic; active/high-volume, low immediate hiding cost; Home overview / Analytics detail; a rate is not a career prediction.
24. **Progress recommended focus:** What deserves attention? Search Health candidate selection, recommendation; high if process concern, otherwise optional, each analytic refresh; active, potentially high hiding cost; Home only if clearly distinguished from task queue; independent from dashboard action priority.
25. **Expanded comparison/process details:** Why this interpretation? Periods, supporting signals, sample warning, active-process coverage, server derivation; high explanatory value, low first-view urgency, on request; curious/uncertain states, moderate hiding cost if a recommendation lacks any explanation; Home disclosure / Analytics detail.
26. **Loading, error, retry, route notice:** What can I trust now? Query/mutation states, temporary system fact; high at occurrence, immediate; all states, high hiding cost; Home transient; independent dashboard/analytics requests can represent different snapshots or fail separately.

## 9. Data capability matrix

- **Where am I? — Available for recorded workspace.** Status counts, historical milestones, active status, interviews, settings. Not a complete real-world search because external opportunities are unknown. Architecture/presentation issue if counts are mistaken for workload.
- **What is overdue? — Available only for an explicit recorded follow-up date.** `follow_up_date`, saved IANA time zone, local today are sufficient; a candidate-owned undated step is not literally overdue. Stale stage age is a heuristic, not a deadline. Semantic/model issue when these are merged under “Overdue.”
- **What is upcoming? — Available, with bounded coverage.** Scheduled interview timestamps and due dates exist; Home interview list is capped at five and due follow-up query at 100. Exact universe may need different query semantics for count. The server should remain owner of date logic.
- **What recently became actionable? — Partially available/derivable.** Timestamp threshold crossing can be calculated from existing dates, but event timing and previous delivered state are not stored as a Home transition record. “Became actionable since last visit” is not currently reliable.
- **What changed since my previous visit? — Not available as a reliable Home answer.** Application `updated_at`, append-only per-application activity, interview/note events, and profile `last_login_at` exist. However, `DynamoUserRepository.get_or_create` sets `last_login_at` when creating the profile and does not update it on later reads; Home has no usable last-seen snapshot or visit boundary. Not every time-driven change is an edit. The data can support *some* history reconstruction; exact material-since-last-visit requires a product/data decision.
- **Which opportunity stalled? — Partially available.** `stage_entered_at` plus status can flag age; employer response expectation, hiring process timeline, and candidate intent are missing. The 14/21/9-day heuristics disagree by surface. Stage age is not proof of a stalled employer process.
- **What should I do next? — Partially available.** Candidate responsibility, follow-up date, interview proximity, stage, checklist progress and route actions exist. Opportunity importance, outside constraints, opportunity-specific consequences, and candidate preference are not modeled. A plausible suggestion is possible; a universally correct top action is not.
- **What is waiting on an employer? — Partially available.** `next_step_responsibility=EMPLOYER` and status/context can identify recorded waits; stale or missing next steps may be unknown rather than employer-owned. Home's current no-action summary does not explain this distinction.
- **What materially changed in pipeline? — Derivable in limited aggregate sense.** Analytics period comparisons and historical milestone timestamps exist. Event-level “material to *this candidate* since last visit” is not established; selected-period differences are not visit deltas.
- **What is approaching a preparation deadline? — Partially available.** Interview schedule and checklist state exist on Interviews; no universal preparation deadline, required effort estimate, or personal availability. Within-24h is a product heuristic.
- **What completed action no longer matters? — Partially available.** Completion mutations and activity exist; Home re-queries after mutation. It cannot know whether follow-up completion resolves all related concern or whether the candidate acted outside HireFlux.
- **What is most important? — Not reliably available as an absolute claim.** A deterministic product sort exists, but not candidate utility/intent. This is a model/authority limit, not merely a visual design gap.

Keep three defect classes separate: *architecture* (too many independent Home claims), *data/model* (no reliable last-visit material-change or personal importance model), and *presentation/semantics* (“Overdue” applied to stale and synthesized today tasks; priority wording without evidence distinction). A different card arrangement alone would not solve the latter two.

## 10. Priority-system audit

There is **no single authoritative cross-Home priority model**. There are several valid but separately scoped systems:

1. Dashboard action generation (`insights.py:65-105,558-633`): dated follow-ups, undated candidate next steps, stage-age heuristic, and interviews within 24h. Priority HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW then due key. Explicit due follow-ups are bounded by schedule query limit 100; upcoming interviews by five.
2. Home header (`DashboardPage.tsx:273-300,365-369`): journey copy order differs from CTA order. Two interviews outrank overdue for chapter description; overdue outranks interview for primary CTA. This is not one coherent rank; it is two separate purposes that are currently not distinguished strongly enough.
3. Home grouping (`DashboardPage.tsx:149-166`): `FOLLOW_UP_TODAY`→Today, `INTERVIEW_SOON`→Upcoming, everything else→Overdue. `STALE_APPLICATION` is therefore mislabeled Overdue despite LOW priority and no contractual deadline. The three columns are group order, not global rank order.
4. Coming next (`DashboardPage.tsx:396-401`): first scheduled interview and an overdue/today count summary, plus fixed exploration links. “Next” is partly chronological and partly generic.
5. Progress recommended focus (`progress_narrative.py:212-235`): chooses among Search Health actionable candidates by semantic type, numeric priority and code, from an Analytics request. Search Health (`search_health.py:18-40,790+`) uses its own thresholds, categories, evidence/sample rules and four-card cap; Progress performance headline is built from separate uncapped comparison candidates. This focus is strategic/process advice, not identical to a dashboard task.
6. Interviews (`resource_services.py:882-925`): server workflow distinguishes PREPARE, UPCOMING, IMMINENT (4h), MISSED, FOLLOW_UP, etc., and owns detailed interview action. Home uses a simpler 24h `INTERVIEW_SOON` rule.

Potential future signals: **implemented** recorded dates, local today, interview time, status/stage age, candidate/employer responsibility, note/checklist history; **derivable** elapsed overdue duration and time-to-interview; **partial** consequence of inaction and employer-response expectation; **unavailable** relative opportunity importance, candidate availability/intent, outside commitments, reversibility and outcome influence. Stage 1 does not rank or weight them.

## 11. Temporal information model

- A recorded follow-up passes future→today→overdue by comparing a date-only value with the workspace-local date (`insights.py:65-86,558-581`). The browser shows the saved time zone or UTC fallback. A date-only concept must not be UTC-converted as an instant.
- A candidate-owned next step without a follow-up date enters Today's group immediately through a synthetic `due_date=local_today`; its temporal label is not a recorded deadline (`insights.py:583-603`).
- A scheduled interview is returned in the upcoming list, enters dashboard action at ≤24h, while Interviews' detailed workflow calls it imminent at ≤4h and can distinguish missed/past states (`resource_services.py:128,882-925`). Different names can be coherent if scope is explicit, but Home currently presents them as competing urgency cues.
- An APPLIED or SCREENING record enters dashboard staleness at ≥14 elapsed days from `stage_entered_at` (`insights.py:605-622`); Search Health uses APPLIED 21, SCREENING 14, INTERVIEW 9 days (`search_health.py:35-40`). Both are heuristics, neither a due date. Repository updated time may change without resetting stage age, so “recently touched” and “stale” can describe one record simultaneously.
- “Quiet stretch” uses zero submissions across the latest **two weekly trend points**, not inactivity since the last visit (`DashboardPage.tsx:273-300`). The trend is fixed at eight weeks, while performance is selectable 30d/90d/all and process health is current whole-workspace. A range change does not necessarily change all parts of the Home story.
- Recalculation occurs on server request/refetch, query invalidation after mutations, and eligible remount/staleness; there is no midnight timer or window-focus refetch (`queryClient.ts:4-23`). A Home left open across midnight can display a now-stale Today/Overdue classification until refreshed. DynamoDB schedule-index reads are eventually consistent (`docs/dynamodb-access-patterns.md:83`). These are temporal freshness limitations, not evidence of a specific live failure.

## 12. Redundancy and ownership map

- **“What needs attention?”** header count/CTA → Coming next summary → Action Center group/badge → Progress focus/process health. They do not always use the same population or ordering. The candidate may need to determine whether a Progress “focus” outranks the Action Center's top item. This is the clearest cross-panel reconciliation burden.
- **“What should I do next?”** header CTA → fixed Coming next links → Action Center task actions → Progress recommended-focus action → Search tour next step. Tour is onboarding, not operational work; fixed links are navigation, not recommendations. Their placement/phrasing can nonetheless make them compete.
- **“What changed?”** recent `updated_at` records → Progress period comparison → per-record activity on detail routes. These answer edit recency, aggregate trend, and historical event, respectively; they are *not* true duplicates. The problematic overlap is the shared change language without aligned time/scope.
- **“Where am I?”** journey chapter → active/total counts → milestone path. Productively complementary if their scopes remain explicit; total tracked is not active workload.
- **“Which opportunity?”** next interview → interview-soon action → overdue follow-up for possibly the same application → recent record. Same application can validly appear multiple times, but its different roles need reconciliation.

## 13. Cognitive friction map

Each finding names the friction, evidence, candidate work/consequence, evidence class, and confidence.

1. **Priority reconciliation:** live header, Coming next, collapsed Action Center, and Progress offer several next/focus claims; candidate must compare unlike ranking systems before acting; likely slower or less confident triage. **Strong product inference; high confidence in the integration task, unmeasured magnitude.**
2. **Semantic urgency inflation:** `STALE_APPLICATION` falls into Overdue; undated candidate step becomes due Today. Candidate may treat heuristics as missed commitments, risking unnecessary interruption or distrust. **Strong product inference; high** for label mismatch, moderate for harm.
3. **Hidden operational queue:** live 10-action center is below first fold and initially collapsed; candidate must remember header count while scanning intervening tour/recency/next sections. **Strong product inference; high** for path, low for actual task-error rate.
4. **False change scent:** “What changed recently” lists `updated_at` records, no last-visit comparison; returning candidate must inspect records to discover whether anything material changed. **Strong product inference; high** for data gap, moderate for experienced cost.
5. **Time-horizon switching:** current commitments, “recent” edits, 30-day performance and all-workspace process health appear in one narrative. Candidate must hold scopes in mind to avoid conflating a strategic trend with today's task. **Strong product inference; moderate.**
6. **Onboarding interruption:** undismissed tour sits before recent movement and operational work in live state; useful for learning, potentially costly in high urgency. **Hypothesis requiring testing; moderate** for placement, low for behavioral harm.
7. **Ambiguous no-action:** zero dashboard action count can coexist with a strategic focus from Analytics; a candidate may infer that “caught up” conflicts with a next-step recommendation. **Strong product inference; moderate** because the concrete zero example was an intentionally inconsistent mock.
8. **Sample/authority confusion:** a server-generated performance headline can sound prescriptive in low-sample or fictional demo data despite caveats. Candidate may over-trust it. **Hypothesis requiring testing; low** without comprehension data.

These are not deductions from “seven ± two,” fold dogma, or a generic minimalism heuristic. The observable mechanism is cross-region integration and semantic scope switching.

## 14. Current attention path

Observed 1280×900 live path: **enter Home → search chapter + primary CTA + whole-workspace/range counts → tour → recently updated records → next conversation + generic continuation links → collapsed Action Center summary → optional open of overdue/today/upcoming work → progress interpretation + separate focus → optional comparison detail.** Friction boundaries: chapter versus primary CTA; onboarding versus urgent work; recently *touched* versus actually *changed*; chronological next interview versus overdue work; operational queue versus strategic recommendation. The first viewport does not need to contain everything, but the candidate must decide whether an unseen queue is more important than visible tour/recency.

## 15. First-view responsibility

Before scrolling, Home should establish the workspace and the limits of the recorded search, whether a known consequential commitment exists, whether the next move is candidate-owned or waiting, and whether a suggested action is fact-backed or provisional. It should not promise a complete priority ranking or a since-last-visit delta it cannot know. This is an **information responsibility**, not a requirement for any element to be above a pixel boundary.

## 16. Degraded, empty and uncertain states

- **Zero applications:** source selects foundation branch, Add application, no recent/interview, zero Action Center and caught-up text. The constructed dashboard-only probe confirmed this presentation but not a coherent all-zero product experience. Progress must avoid pretending fixture analytics are the same workspace snapshot.
- **Active but no urgent tasks / all employer-waiting:** Action Center says caught up and Coming next says no immediate attention; generic Add/Pipeline/Patterns links remain. Source has `next_step_responsibility`, but Home does not directly summarize waiting ownership. No action is a healthy state, not a failed one.
- **No interviews:** explicit no-interview message and pipeline link, rather than an invented interview task.
- **Missing dates / next steps:** undated candidate-owned step is synthesized Today; absent responsibility+date becomes Analytics process-health missing count. Unknown should not be represented as a recorded deadline.
- **Completed/rescheduled follow-up:** Home shows temporary completion/emphasis; mutation invalidates dashboard/analytics; eventual indexed reads can lag. A resolved task may remain briefly in an old response. Do not equate UI transition with a guaranteed instant projection update.
- **Archived/rejected/withdrawn:** archived excluded from recent/action staleness, active pursuit excludes terminal states, total tracked includes them. Activity history still exists on the record. A recently edited terminal record may occupy recency without being actionable.
- **Dashboard unavailable:** full Home skeleton then ErrorPanel/retry; no authoritative cross-workspace priority claim should be inferred. **Analytics unavailable while dashboard succeeds:** milestone fallback + explicit interpretation error/retry; factual counts remain.
- **Two independently timed responses:** Home checks Analytics range before use, but does not check matching `generated_at` or a shared snapshot. The synthetic probe exposed contradictory counts; real eventual consistency/cache timing could cause a smaller version. This is a **risk**, not a measured production incidence.
- **Malformed data:** API responses are Zod-validated (`frontend/src/api/workspace.ts`/schemas), so malformed fields should fail a query rather than silently become a recommendation. Not separately tested in this audit.
- **Partially completed tour / changed workspace:** tour uses browser session storage, while Action Center expansion preference is workspace-scoped. The tour can be out of sync with durable backend task meaning; it should remain labeled exploration.

## 17. Product authority and trust model

- **Fact:** recorded interview time, saved follow-up date, current status, known responsibility, counts with stated scope. The product can state these firmly, subject to freshness and quota bounds.
- **Derived state:** “due today” for a *recorded date*, days in stage, historical rate with denominator, checklist completion. State the derivation/time zone and avoid global outcome claims.
- **Inference:** “stale,” “quiet stretch,” “worth watching,” “your chapter.” These are heuristics over incomplete context. Do not phrase as employer intent or candidate failure.
- **Recommendation:** prepare, review, follow up, add a next step. Explain the recorded signals and let candidate judgment override; a candidate might reasonably value two opportunities differently.
- **Strong prescription:** “this is most important.” Not justified globally: HireFlux lacks personal opportunity value, external commitments, availability, reversibility and outcome consequences. Even a dated overdue item may be lower priority than an unrecorded interview or deadline. Stage 2 should preserve confidence/unknown distinctions, not invent a new absolute rank.

## 18. Information governance model

- **Persistent Home responsibility:** workspace identity/scope, recorded consequential commitments, current candidate-versus-employer ownership where known, an honest no-action state, and enough explanation to interpret the suggested next step.
- **Contextual Home responsibility:** stage/search orientation, next scheduled conversation, material event summary *only when knowable*, sample-aware progress, and onboarding while genuinely useful.
- **Temporary visibility:** loading/errors, mutation status, route-created notices, stale-data caveats and refresh uncertainty.
- **Optional access:** full tour steps, comparison evidence, strategic patterns, record history and broader trend detail.
- **Dedicated routes:** application editing/activity, interview preparation, pipeline movement, deep analytics, settings. Home may link into them.
- **No unsupported Home claim:** exact changes since last visit, employer intent, universal top priority, candidate opportunity valuation, guaranteed outcome, or “overdue” without an actual date/commitment. The governance question for each concept is: why must the candidate consider this *now*, and what is lost if it waits?

## 19. Research findings and limits

- **Established evidence / cautious transfer:** [Endsley, *Toward a Theory of Situation Awareness in Dynamic Systems* (1995)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1518/001872095779049543) models situation awareness as perception, comprehension and projection in dynamic work. HireFlux also has changing state, but it is not a safety-critical control room. Product implication: distinguish known current facts from interpretation and near-future commitments. Whether this improves job-search performance remains untested.
- **Established evidence / cautious transfer:** [Sweller, *Cognitive Load During Problem Solving* (1988)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1207/s15516709cog1202_4) found processing demands can compete with learning in studied problem-solving conditions. It does **not** prescribe card counts or prove this Home is overloaded. Strong product inference: multiple unmatched priority claims impose an integration task. The resulting time/error cost needs HireFlux-specific testing.
- **Established evidence / cautious transfer:** [Lee and See, *Trust in Automation: Designing for Appropriate Reliance* (2004)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1518/hfes.46.1.50_30392) frames appropriate reliance as a calibration problem. HireFlux is a recommendation interface, not a fully autonomous controller. Implication: expose why a suggestion exists and do not pass a heuristic as fact. Actual trust effects require candidate research.
- **UX convention, not a law:** progressive disclosure can keep optional analytic evidence available without forcing it into the initial scan. Existing disclosures already demonstrate this, but whether the current defaults are correct is a hypothesis.
- **Rejected folklore:** neither “everything above the fold,” “7±2 cards,” “three clicks,” nor generic Z/F scanning rules establish Home's correct architecture. The relevant test is whether candidates can accurately orient, identify meaningful commitments and explain their action.

## 21. Unresolved questions and hypotheses

1. Do candidates read “What changed recently” as a last-visit claim, or correctly understand it as recently edited records? Test comprehension, not wording preference alone.
2. Does seeing a collapsed Action Center below orientation delay correct urgent action, or do users use the header CTA effectively? Observe first clicks and scan paths.
3. Which operational signals do candidates consider genuinely consequential? Due date alone may not settle priority.
4. How often do candidates accurately record responsibility and dates? Missingness could make any priority layer unreliable.
5. What should count as a “visit” in a 24-hour demo? No Home visit boundary is defined; the existing `last_login_at` field is not updated after profile creation and cannot supply one.
6. How often do index/query timing differences create visible contradictory snapshots? Synthetic response proves possibility, not frequency.
7. Is the Search Tour helpful in an active high-volume demo, or does its placement distract? Current live view cannot answer.
8. Do users distinguish strategic Analytics focus from an immediate Action Center task? Requires comprehension and task testing.
9. How should Home signal incomplete/capped data (five interviews, 100 due follow-ups) without overstating coverage? This is a product question, not Stage 1 implementation.

## 22. Stage 1 decision ledger

- **Established enough for Stage 2:** Home's job is reorientation plus defensible next action; multiple independently scoped priority claims exist; current “recent” is updated-record recency, not last-visit change; documented temporal labels are not consistently literal; facts and recommendations need distinct authority. Evidence: F1–F5. Implication: Stage 2 must assign conceptual ownership and preserve uncertainty, not merely reshuffle cards.
- **Directionally supported:** stable mental model with state-adaptive emphasis; quiet/no-action as a healthy outcome; keeping deep analytics optional; first-view responsibility to establish consequential exceptions before exploration. Evidence: source branches, live first-viewport ordering, HCI transfer. Implication: compare alternatives instead of freezing a layout.
- **Requires validation:** ideal degree of Action Center prominence, whether tour should appear in the active state, whether candidate time-to-action improves under unified ownership, whether Home should include any material-change digest. Evidence: no candidate study and limited runtime states. Implication: prototype and test with realistic tasks before treating as settled.
- **Blocked by missing product/data capability:** reliable since-last-visit material delta, universal personal opportunity ranking, employer intent, external commitments, explicit preparation deadline. Evidence: current models and absence of a visit/snapshot/intent contract. Implication: Stage 2 must either scope these claims down or separately request product/data work; it must not fake them in UI.

## 23. Success measures for a later comparison

Use representative new, interview-stage, high-volume and quiet/waiting scenarios with the **same underlying data** for current versus future Home. Record: time to identify a defensible highest-consequence recorded action; first-click route/action correctness against scenario-specific expert rubric; number of information regions consulted before decision; ability to explain the evidence and distinguish fact from suggestion; detection of a truly material change versus a trivial update; appropriate recognition of “no action now”; and perceived decision confidence/workload. Include false urgency, missed commitment and route bouncing as error categories. Do not treat fewer clicks or less scrolling as success alone. Hypotheses: clearer conceptual ownership reduces cross-panel inspection and improves explanation without suppressing commitments; an honest change model improves meaningful-change detection; calibrated recommendations preserve or improve trust versus stronger unsupported prescriptions. No percentage targets are asserted before baseline measurement.

## 24. Smallest safe Stage 2 inputs

1. Design Home around recorded situation, consequential exception, ownership, and defensible action—not around completing a card inventory.
2. Resolve conceptual ownership among header action, Coming next, Action Center, and Progress focus. They may coexist only with clear scope/authority distinction.
3. Preserve literal date facts; do not label undated tasks or age heuristics as due/overdue facts.
4. Treat “recently touched,” “since last visit,” and “material change” as different capabilities. Do not promise the latter two without an explicit data contract.
5. Preserve a legitimate no-action/waiting state and separate operational work from optional exploration and strategic analytics.
6. Validate priority comprehension and first-action decisions with candidates before freezing Stage 2 architecture. No Stage 2 architecture, UI design, or recommendation algorithm is specified here.
